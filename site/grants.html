Grant letters with free AI

Grants are paperwork, and paperwork is where AI shines. Free AI can help you structure, draft, and polish a grant proposal — but you supply the facts, the budget, and the funder's requirements.

What AI can do

  • Turn your notes into a narrative case statement.
  • Match your language to the funder's priorities.
  • Expand a one-sentence idea into a full proposal section.
  • Format headings, tables, and page numbers.

The workflow

  1. Gather your facts before you touch AI — funder name, mission, project summary, problem, goals, budget, timeline, key people.
  2. Write a one-paragraph case statement — your organization's story.
  3. Prompt the AI with your facts and ask it to write sections (executive summary, problem statement, project description, goals, budget table, timeline).
  4. Fill the gaps — the AI will leave [INSERT] markers. Fill each one with a real fact.
  5. Check before you send — every number matches your budget, every date matches the deadline, tone matches the funder.

Prompt template

I am writing a grant proposal for a nonprofit. Use only the facts I give you.

Organization: [name]
Mission: [mission]
Funder priority: [paste the funder's stated priority]
Project: [project name and summary]
Problem: [what problem, with your example]
Goal: [what you will achieve]
Budget: [total amount]
Timeline: [start, end, reporting date]

Write a grant proposal with these sections:
1. Executive Summary (150 words)
2. Problem Statement (200 words)
3. Project Description (300 words)
4. Goals and Evaluation
5. Budget Overview (use a table)
6. Timeline

Write in plain, confident English. Do not invent facts, numbers, or programs I did not provide. If I did not give you a fact, write "[INSERT]".

Privacy note

Grant budgets can be sensitive. Use a local model for the budget section. Do not paste donor lists, individual giving history, or private financial details into any hosted service.
